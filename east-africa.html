East Africa

East Africa: the grandest wildlife stage on earth.

East Africa safari planning: Kenya, Tanzania, the great migration and gorilla trekking in Rwanda and Uganda.

Kenya

Big cats, big herds and the Mara River crossings — Kenya is the classic East African safari, with excellent guiding and varied landscapes. The Masai Mara delivers the migration's dramatic river crossings roughly July–October, and it pairs beautifully with Amboseli's elephant herds against Mount Kilimanjaro.

Tanzania

The Serengeti's endless plains and the Ngorongoro Crater's concentrated wildlife — the heart of the great migration's range. Tanzania offers the full arc: calving season in the southern Serengeti (January–March), the Grumeti and Mara crossings mid-year, and the stunning Ngorongoro Crater year-round.

Gorilla trekking

A completely different encounter: trekking through mist forest to spend an hour with mountain gorillas in Rwanda or Uganda. Life-list travel — and easy to combine with a savanna safari for the ultimate East Africa trip. Permits are limited and should be secured well ahead.

Follow the herds, not the calendar

The herds move on their own schedule, not ours. Where you go in East Africa should follow when you can travel — the migration's location shifts through the year, and picking the right area for your dates is exactly the kind of call I help you get right.

East vs. Southern Africa — my honest take

Travelers ask me this constantly. East Africa gives you scale and spectacle — the migration, endless savanna, the greatest wildlife concentrations on earth. Southern Africa gives you intimacy — private reserves, superb guiding, sightings shared with a handful of vehicles. Choose East Africa for the migration, vast herds and gorilla trekking; choose Southern Africa for private reserves and fewer crowds. And combine regions only when the trip earns it — more moving around isn't always more safari.
