Not every safari has four wheels

A traditional game drive is only the beginning. The safari you choose can be exciting, peaceful, completely immersive — or all three.

From Irene's original Africa Friday post — Aug 21, 2026

Walk through the bush with an expert guide — suddenly every footprint and sound matters.

Glide through Botswana's Okavango Delta in a mokoro, a traditional canoe that puts you almost at water level.

Watch the plains wake up from a hot-air balloon, or take a boat safari as elephants come down to drink.

Even traveling between camps becomes part of the experience when you fly by small bush plane or helicopter.

Which one would you try first? I will take all of them.

What each one actually feels like

Walking safari — Slower, closer, and focused on tracks, plants, and birds you would miss from a vehicle. Best for travelers comfortable walking at a steady pace with a guide.

Mokoro / canoe safari — Calm, quiet, and low to the water in delta areas. Best for water, birds, and a peaceful pace, rather than covering a lot of ground.

Boat safari — Animals come to the water, so you see elephants, hippos, and birds from a different viewpoint, often in the middle of the day between drives.

Balloon, bush plane, and helicopter — A balloon gives you the plains from above at dawn. A bush plane or helicopter is not just transport — it is how you see the scale of the landscape between camps.

Most travelers do not choose just one. A typical mix is game drives in the morning and evening, with a walk, a boat trip, or a canoe outing in between. Check age and fitness rules before you book — many walking safaris set a minimum age, often around 12, and a mokoro or balloon has weight and mobility limits that vary by operator. Those rules are not just paperwork; they decide whether an activity is actually available to your group.
