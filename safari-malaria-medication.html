Can you go on safari without malaria medication?

Yes — South Africa has some excellent malaria-free safari areas. You do not necessarily have to sacrifice safari quality — you just need to know which safari area is right for you.

From Irene's original Africa Friday post — Oct 9, 2026

Madikwe — Big Five, beautiful lodges, excellent game viewing and especially known for wild dogs.

Eastern Cape — Think Shamwari, Kwandwe and other private reserves. Very good safari experience and easy to combine with Cape Town and the Garden Route.

Waterberg — Beautiful, malaria-free and convenient from Johannesburg. There are some excellent reserves here, but choosing the right one really matters.

Pilanesberg — Big Five and easy from Johannesburg. A good option if you are short on time.

And then there is Greater Kruger / Sabi Sands — still hard to beat for wildlife density and leopard sightings.

So do you have to sacrifice safari quality to stay malaria-free? Not necessarily. You just need to know which safari area is right for you.

What to check before you choose

The four areas above are the malaria-free options Irene listed. Greater Kruger / Sabi Sands is listed separately in her post — it is the high-density wildlife alternative, not one of the malaria-free areas, so it is a different decision if medication is a concern for you.

Malaria risk also varies by season and by exact itinerary, not just by country. A dry-season trip, a stopover, or a lodge in a different area can change the picture, which is why a trip that was low-risk in one season is not automatically low-risk in another. Classifications can also change over time, so check the current status for your exact areas at booking time, not just what was true for a previous trip.

Choosing a malaria-free area does not remove all travel-health planning. Ask your clinician about the other routine health questions for that trip as well, so malaria is not the only item checked.

For any itinerary, talk to a travel clinician early, about the specific areas and dates on your trip, and use bite prevention as well — repellent, covered clothing in the evening, and the sleeping arrangements recommended for that area.

This guide is general travel information, not medical advice. Talk to a qualified travel clinician about your own health, medications, and itinerary before you travel.
